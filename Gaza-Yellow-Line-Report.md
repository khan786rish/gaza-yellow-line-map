# Gaza's yellow line — what the plan says, and what the map already shows

*Written for people in Gaza trying to work out what comes next. October 2026. Every claim is sourced at the bottom.*

---

## The short version

A year ago, on 10 October 2025, a ceasefire was announced. Israeli forces pulled back to a line marked in yellow on military maps, holding about half the Strip. That was supposed to be step one of a process that ended with Israeli withdrawal, a Palestinian technocratic government, and reconstruction.

None of that has happened. Instead, the yellow line has moved outward, not back. Israel now holds somewhere between 60% and 70% of Gaza. Rafah, a city of a quarter of a million people, has been demolished almost entirely. More than 1,380 Palestinians have been killed since the "ceasefire" began. And the plan that is supposed to govern all of this — the Board of Peace roadmap — is written in a way that lets the current map stay frozen indefinitely, as long as "disarmament" is never certified as complete.

Here is the detail.

## What the map shows right now

**The yellow line.** This is the line Israeli forces withdrew to on 10 October 2025. At the time it covered roughly 50–53% of the Strip — everything east of a line running from Beit Hanoun down past Shujaiya, cutting east of Khan Yunis, and wrapping around Rafah. Since then it has only moved one way. On 28 May 2026 Netanyahu ordered the army to expand the held area to about 70%. Satellite analysis by the Associated Press, published 7 October 2026, found a miles-long earth berm built along the line and at least 18 new military outposts constructed *after* the ceasefire — bringing the total to more than 50. You do not build berms and permanent outposts on a line you plan to leave.

**The corridors.** Three military corridors slice across what remains. Netzarim cuts the north off from the center. Morag cuts Khan Yunis off from Rafah. Philadelphi runs along the Egyptian border. Together with the yellow line they partition Gaza into fenced compartments. Al Jazeera's mapping unit puts effective Israeli control, counting the corridors, at 63–66% of the territory.

**The compressed zone.** About 2.1 million people — nearly the entire population — are now packed into roughly 40% of the land: Gaza City and the northern towns, the central camps, the western half of Khan Yunis, and the coastal strip around al-Mawasi. Al-Mawasi, the area repeatedly designated a "humanitarian zone," has been bombed repeatedly anyway.

**Rafah is gone.** The AP satellite analysis found Rafah — home to about 250,000 people before the war — has been almost completely razed since Israeli forces took it. Not damaged. Erased: neighborhoods flattened block by block. George Galloway put the point in seven words on 16 October, over ground footage of the demolition: "Erasing the traces will not erase the truth." The video, from the MoatsTV offshoot Hats Off, describes Israeli machinery changing the landscape of Rafah — "erasing traces of genocide." Taken together with the AP satellite confirmation, the same act is now documented from the street and from orbit: not only a city destroyed, but the evidence of how it died being physically removed. It is also why talk of people "returning home" to the south currently means returning to a gravel plain inside an Israeli-held zone.

**The firing never stopped.** The Gaza Health Ministry's figure, carried by OCHA, is over 1,381 killed since the ceasefire began — shot near the line, hit by drones and strikes inside the Palestinian zone, killed at aid points. Forensic Architecture, which has been documenting attacks for three years, describes the situation bluntly: the ceasefire is normalising the continuation of the genocide. Their public database (gaza.forensic-architecture.org/database) logs it incident by incident.

## What the plan on paper actually says

The governing document is the Board of Peace's "Roadmap for Completing the Implementation of President Trump's Comprehensive Peace Plan in Gaza," published 30–31 July 2026, with an accompanying explanatory document. It sits on top of UN Security Council Resolution 2803 (17 November 2025). In plain words, its 15 points say:

- **A Palestinian technocratic committee (NCAG)** is supposed to take over civil administration. It entered Gaza under Clause 2 with a 14-day timetable for Israel to complete its Sharm el-Sheikh commitments. That timetable was never met.
- **"One Authority, One Law, One Weapon."** All weapons — militia, police, heavy, personal — are to be handed to the NCAG only. None to Israel. A verification body (IVC) decides, sector by sector, whether an area counts as disarmed.
- **An International Stabilization Force (ISF)** deploys as a buffer between the two sides. The roadmap is explicit that it is *not* there to police Palestinians. Its staging base at Kerem Shalom was set up in August 2026.
- **Israeli withdrawal is phased and conditional.** Each phase of withdrawal happens only after the IVC certifies disarmament in that sector. Clause 13 states "no one will be forced to leave the Strip."
- **Reconstruction** is supervised by the Board of Peace and the NCAG — not by any elected Palestinian body. A $2.45 billion reconstruction blueprint was announced on 23 September 2026.

Nikolay Mladenov, the plan's senior political figure, spelled out the sequencing in a public thread in May 2026: sector by sector, an area gets services and reconstruction only after it is cleared and certified. Read that alongside the map and the logic is hard to miss — the parts of Gaza inside the yellow line are not scheduled for anything at all, because as far as the plan's machinery is concerned they are not in play.

## Why nothing is moving

Three blockages keep the map frozen.

**The disarmament deadlock.** Hamas will not hand over weapons while Israeli troops hold most of Gaza and keep killing people. Israel will not withdraw until disarmament is certified. The roadmap makes withdrawal conditional on disarmament — so each side's position feeds the other's, and the yellow line sits where it sits. "Phase two" of the ceasefire was formally launched on 14 January 2026 by Steve Witkoff. It has never actually been activated on the ground.

**The money does not exist.** As of May 2026 the Board of Peace's Gaza fund held zero dollars, despite $17 billion in announced pledges. Reconstruction is the main lever the plan has to offer Palestinians, and the lever is not attached to anything.

**Israeli politics.** Israel joined the Board of Peace on 21 January 2026, days before Trump's Davos ultimatum pushed the process forward. But Israel holds elections on 27 October 2026. No Israeli government campaigning in October 2026 has any incentive to be seen pulling out of Gaza — and the far-right members of the coalition want the held zone kept, settled, or both. Meanwhile the "Hamas-free humanitarian zones" concept floated in June 2026 points the other way: concentrating the population in certified-cleared coastal enclaves while the rest stays under military control.

## What this means where you live

**If your home is inside the yellow line** — Rafah, Khuza'a, eastern Khan Yunis, Shujaiya, Beit Hanoun, the eastern border belt: the plan contains no mechanism and no date for you to go back. The land is being physically altered — bermed, bulldozed, outposted. "No one will be forced to leave" is technically true of people who have already left; it says nothing about being allowed back.

**If you are in the compressed western zone:** you are living in the area the plan actually governs. Expect the pressure to arrive through services and aid — certification, clearance, "humanitarian zones" — rather than through new expulsion orders. The roadmap's own logic makes the western strip the holding pen and the showcase at the same time.

**The honest bottom line:** the document says phased withdrawal, no forced displacement, Palestinian administration, reconstruction. The ground says partition — a shrinking Palestinian enclave, an expanded and fortified Israeli-held zone, and a ceasefire that kills people weekly. Until disarmament verification and the funding actually move, the map is the plan.

## The doctrine behind the freeze

If the map makes the plan look permanent, the thinking now coming out of Israel's security establishment explains why.

On 12 October 2026 — two days before the Board of Peace's anniversary celebration — the Institute for National Security Studies (INSS), the Tel Aviv University-affiliated think tank that functions as Israel's strategic brain trust, published a white paper titled "The 'New' National Security Concept: Conceptualization and Questions for Discussion." Its author is Tamir Hayman, INSS's executive director and, from 2018 to 2021, chief of the IDF's Military Intelligence Directorate. This is not a fringe blogger. This is the man who ran Israeli intelligence assessment.

The paper argues that "most Arab states are not genuine nation-states," and that undermining their stability "is viewed as a natural process, one that Israel can manage more effectively than it can contend with strong nation-states, and one that it should even encourage." It goes further: "War is essential to national security and cannot be replaced by a state of peace." Seeking peace "is an expression of weakness." "War is necessary, and preventive war is preferable" — and the "constant presence [of war] is an asset to national security."

Read that next to the roadmap. The plan promises phased withdrawal, disarmament certification, reconstruction. The doctrine published by Israel's leading security institute in the same week holds that war itself is an asset and that stable neighboring states are a threat. Under that doctrine, a frozen yellow line is not a stalled peace process. It is the desired condition: a managed, permanent, low-grade war against a fragmented and dependent Palestinian enclave, while reconstruction money and sovereignty stay theoretical.

It also explains what the peace architecture is actually for. The Board of Peace, the NCAG, the verification body, the stabilization force — none of it is designed to end the conflict. It is designed to administer the conflict indefinitely, at a tempo Israel can sustain, under an international stamp of approval. Norton, who first surfaced the paper, calls the trajectory a descent into "apocalyptic fascism." Whatever label you prefer, the direction is stated in the establishment's own words: the report frames the future as a "permanent Israeli-Arab conflict," rejects peace as weakness, and treats war as a national asset.

The world outside has noticed. Nira Data's 2026 Global Country Perceptions index, published in May, ranked Israel last of all 129 countries surveyed — below North Korea, Afghanistan and Iran. Pew's Spring 2026 Global Attitudes survey found a median of 67% of adults across 36 countries viewing Israel unfavorably, and the share is still climbing in most of them. A state whose published doctrine requires permanent war, and whose standing has collapsed to last place on Earth, is not looking for an exit from the yellow line. It is looking for a way to hold the line forever, at a price fewer and fewer countries are willing to help pay.

One more data point, because it shows where this doctrine came from. A 2012 book by the Israeli-American writer Avi Lipkin ("Return to Mecca") argued that Israel's survival depends on turning Saudi Arabia, Egypt and Iraq into "warring sectarian and ethnic mini-states" — fragmenting the major Arab states through minorities, sectarian conflict and economic crisis, dismantling the Egyptian army, controlling Gulf oil. At the time it read as messianic fringe. Fourteen years later, a former head of IDF Military Intelligence has published a milder-worded version of the same idea at Israel's most influential think tank. The fringe didn't win the argument. It got promoted to doctrine.

Washington supplied its own version the same week. On 13 October, Secretary of State Marco Rubio — one of the men Kushner publicly thanked for delivering the ceasefire — gave a speech on "Western civilization" that praised colonialism, skipped over the extermination of Native Americans, and declared that the West must use military force to "maintain its place at the head of the world." His words: "We choose the unapologetic pursuit of national power." Take the two statements together and the week reads as a matched pair. Tel Aviv says war is an asset and stable Arab states are a threat. Washington says domination is the point and force is the tool. Between them sits a document called a peace plan.

## What to watch

- **27 October 2026** — the Israeli election. Any movement on the line comes after it, not before.
- **The IVC's first sector certification** — if one sector (likely a northern or central pocket) ever gets certified "disarmed," watch whether Israeli forces actually pull back there, and whether money actually arrives. That is the test of the whole document.
- **The Board of Peace fund balance** — pledges are press releases. Transfers are real.
- **The berm and the outposts** — physical facts are the hardest to walk back. If they keep growing, the line is a border.
